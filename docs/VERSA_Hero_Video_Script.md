## 1. The film in one breath

> A world of tiny plastic particles drifting in bottled water, then up to the open sky, where the Chatak drinks only what the sky gives. The rain sinks into the earth of the north, is drawn from the source and handled with care, and is sealed in a white aluminium can. Then the live VERSA can on the website takes over.

The arc runs **problem → the sky's way → the source → the care → the seal**.

- **The client** asked (19 Oct 2026) to open on the microplastic problem first, then begin the bird's story.
- **The designer's** idea was the bird drinking rain, and that same rain being sourced, managed and packed as VERSA.

This script does both.

## 2. How the website uses the video (why the rules exist)

- **It starts inside the can.** On the opening screen, scrolling makes the 3D can tip its lid, open and fill the screen with its opening; the camera dives in. **The video's first frame is that soft, bright light** (K1).
- **Scroll plays it, frame by frame, both ways.** The master is cut into still frames, and scrolling moves through them. A hard cut, a flash, flicker or a fast move looks broken when scrubbed. So every shot hands over to the next through a camera move, and everything moves slowly and steadily.
- **It ends in the page's mist.** The last second is a flat pale grey, `#DFE4E8` (the page colour). The website then brings back its live 3D VERSA can, with the real label ("Meet VERSA. Sealed in aluminium. Never plastic."). That is the packaging reveal, so the AI never has to draw the label.
- **Words are added by the website**, as live text over the video (§8). Nothing is written in the video itself.
- **Two shapes.**
  - Desktop shows the 16:9 frame. The site's words sit on the right third, so keep the subject between 35% and 60% of the width.
  - Phones show a centre 9:16 crop of the same frames, so everything important must sit inside the centre third. Keep the main subject in the upper two-thirds of the height; phones put the words at the bottom.
  - If time allows, also make a native 9:16 master (§10).

## 3. Look and feel

### 3.1 Premium and realistic means

- **Photoreal.** Real light, real water physics, real feathers. Nothing that looks like CGI, illustration or a stock ad.
- **Restraint.** Few elements, lots of air and negative space, slow moves. Think of a luxury nature documentary meeting a high-end product film: the calm of a BBC natural-history sequence, the finish of a premium water advert.
- **High-key and luminous.** Soft, diffused daylight and gentle mist. Shadows are lifted, never crushed to black (except the Chatak's black plumage, which keeps its detail). Highlights stay soft and never clip.
- **One colour family from start to end:** cool silver, mist grey, silver-green woods, slate storm (cool, never purple or violet), clear pale-blue water.
- **Cinematic optics.** Large-format cinema camera, shallow depth of field, 100 mm macro for the small worlds (particles, drops, filling), long lenses for the bird, wide only for the sky.
- **Water is the hero texture.** Slow-motion drops, crowns of splash, clear streams, condensation. Always crystal clear; never muddy or dirty.

### 3.3 Subjects: what each must look like

- **The Chatak (চাতক)** is a **pied cuckoo**, also called the Jacobin cuckoo (*Clamator jacobinus*), about 33 cm long including its tail.
  - Glossy **black upperparts** with a **tall, pointed, swept-back black crest**.
  - **Clean white throat, breast and belly**, and a **small white patch on the black wing** (a white crescent near the wingtips in flight).
  - A **long, graduated black tail with white tips**.
  - Dark eye, slender slightly curved black beak, grey legs.
  - It must not turn into a magpie, a magpie-robin (doyel), a bulbul, a crow or a kingfisher.
  - It perches on a **thin bare acacia branch with small feathery leaflets**.
- **The plastic bottle** is a plain, **unbranded** clear PET water bottle: thin and crinkled, with moulded ridges, a white cap, **no label and no writing**. It must not look like any real brand's bottle shape.
- **The land** is northern Bangladesh in the monsoon (Panchagarh, near Tetulia): **flat** green tea gardens and paddy fields, misty woods of tall thin trees, soft rain. There are no mountains in view; the Himalayas are hidden by monsoon cloud.
- **The facility** is spotless and bright: brushed and polished stainless steel, clear glass pipe sections, white walls, pale grey floor and daylight from tall windows. There are **no people, no signs, no screens and no writing**.
- **The VERSA can and bottle** are **matte white** with bare-aluminium rims and lids (the can) and a silver cap (the bottle), exactly as in the photos and the site's stills.
  - The printed artwork (a navy bird, a blue water swash and the VERSA wordmark) may appear **only turned away or softly out of focus, never readable**.

### K1: through the opening (the first frame)

This one is better made in an image editor than with AI: an exact gradient (`#D6E6F2` at the top, `#F4F7F9` at 60%, `#E6EDF2` at the bottom) with a few blurred specks. If you use AI:

```text
[STYLE BLOCK] Abstract, completely out-of-focus view into soft, bright, cool daylight, as if
```

### K2: the particles

```text
[STYLE BLOCK] Extreme macro inside perfectly still, clear water, lit by one soft shaft of cool
daylight from the upper left. Hundreds of microscopic plastic particles hang suspended at
different depths: tiny translucent shards, thin curled fibres and small round beads, some
with a faint rainbow edge where the light catches them. Only a few particles near the centre
are pin-sharp; the rest melt into soft bokeh. Pale silver-grey background, cool and clinical
yet quietly beautiful. The densest cluster sits in the centre of the frame. 100 mm macro, f/2.8.
```

Check: it reads as particles in water, not dust in air and not snow. Nothing is coloured, and nothing looks like rubbish.

```text
[STYLE BLOCK] A plain, unbranded clear plastic water bottle (thin crinkled PET with moulded
```

## 9. Claims: what the video may and may not say

**It may show:**

- tiny plastic particles in water from a plastic bottle (a known, published finding);
- the Chatak drinking rain (folklore, told as a story);
- rain sinking into the earth (if the water is groundwater; see the questions below);
- a clean facility;
- water sealed in aluminium.

**It must not say or imply:**

- that VERSA contains **zero microplastics** or is "plastic-free". Cans and bottles usually have a thin inner coating; the client must confirm its type first;
- any **health claim** ("microplastics cause…", "healthier", "detox");
- that rain or VERSA is "100% pure";
- any **specific treatment step** (reverse osmosis, UV, minerals, depth of a well, number of filtration stages) until the client confirms it;
- **another brand**, by name, logo or recognisable bottle shape;
- **people** (AI faces and hands look wrong, and real people need releases).

**Questions for the client** (before generating S6–S8, or at least before approving them):

1. **The source.** Where does VERSA water come from: a deep well (aquifer), a spring, or something else, and where exactly? S6 shows rain sinking through the earth, which suits groundwater.
2. **The process.** Which treatment steps does the plant use? S7–S8 stay generic until we know.
3. **The microplastics line.** Are they happy to cite the 2024 study (§8, note ¹)?
4. **The inner coating.** What lines the can and the bottle (for example BPA-NI)? This decides the "not plastic" wording.
5. **Photos of the plant**, to use as references, so S7–S9 look like their own factory.
6. **The place.** Is it Tetulia in Panchagarh? (Their original story said "Umm Lajj".)

## 10. Tools, editing and delivery

### Tools (October 2026; check the current options inside the tool)

- **Google Veo 3.1** (in Flow or Gemini). The most realistic option, with first and last frame, up to three reference images, 4, 6 or 8 s clips, 24 fps, 16:9 and 9:16, up to 4K. First choice for S4 and S5 (the bird) and the water macros.
- **Kling 3.0.** Start and end frames, element references, clips of about 3–15 s, strong liquid motion. A good alternative or second opinion.
- **Runway Gen-4.5.** The most precise camera and keyframe control; useful if a move needs exact steering.
- Use a **paid plan** so commercial use is allowed.

### Editing (DaVinci Resolve, free, or CapCut)

1. **Assemble.** Put S1…S9 in order on one track and trim them to the times in §4. Joins share a keyframe, so cut on that frame. Use a short 6–8 frame dissolve only if a join still jumps.
2. **Speed.** If a move feels fast, slow it (optical flow); scrolling exaggerates speed.
3. **Stabilise** only where there is jitter.
4. **Grade** every clip to the keyframes:
   - lift the shadows and keep the whites soft;
   - cool the highlights slightly;
   - keep the storm slate, not purple;
   - make the last second a flat `#DFE4E8` (check 223, 228, 232 with the colour picker) and hold it for 1 s.
5. **Remove all audio.**
